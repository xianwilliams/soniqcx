The boundary matters

What Usually Stays Internal

  • Decisions about product direction and what is technically in scope
  • Authority to approve exceptions or changes outside the agreed procedures
  • The final escalation point for issues the operation cannot resolve
  • Any function that requires context or authority that cannot be shared

Decision signals

When Each Approach Makes More Sense

In-House May Fit Better When…

  • The technical scope is constantly changing and hard to document
  • Resolution depends on private, informal context
  • Support is tightly coupled to ongoing product development
  • The volume does not justify a structured operation

Outsourcing May Fit Better When…

  • The issue categories and procedures can be defined
  • Troubleshooting is repeatable enough to document
  • Escalation boundaries are clear
  • The outcome can be measured and attributed
  • The business wants accountability tied to results, not hours

Clear answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Is outsourced technical support better than an in-house team?
Neither is automatically better. In-house teams have deep, immediate product context, while outsourced operations can provide a defined, scalable troubleshooting function. The right choice depends on the technical scope and how well it can be documented and measured.
What technical support should stay in-house?
Decisions about product direction, final escalation authority, and anything requiring context or authority that cannot be shared usually stay with the client. The engagement defines the boundary.
How does outsourced technical support stay accurate?
Accuracy depends on defining the issue categories, available information, approved procedures, and escalation boundaries up front, then measuring resolution quality against that scope.
Does SONIQCX charge by the hour for technical support?
No. SONIQCX does not charge clients by the hour. Compensation is tied to the agreed performance outcomes of the technical support engagement.
Can an outsourced team escalate into our engineers?
The escalation path is defined for the engagement. The operation can escalate issues outside its scope into the internal technical team with the agreed context.
What makes outsourcing technical support work well?
A clearly defined technical scope, documented procedures, agreed escalation boundaries, and a measurable resolution outcome. When those are in place, an external operation can be held accountable for real results.
Can I keep some technical support in-house and outsource the rest?
The engagement scope is defined for the specific program. SONIQCX can operate within an agreed portion of the technical support function rather than replacing everything.
