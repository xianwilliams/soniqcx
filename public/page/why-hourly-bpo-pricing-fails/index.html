What Is Wrong With Hourly Pricing?

Hourly pricing rewards the delivery of labor, not the delivery of results.

When the provider is paid by the hour, efficiency, resolution, and prevention can all work against the provider's revenue. The client is left carrying the performance risk alone.

The issue is not that hourly providers are bad. The issue is that the commercial model is misaligned with the outcome the client actually wants.

Structural flaws

Four Structural Flaws of Hourly Pricing.

More Problems Means More Hours

Preventable issues drive repeat contacts, which increases staffing and vendor billing.

Efficiency Reduces Vendor Revenue

If fewer agents can handle the work, the vendor loses billable hours, so there is little incentive to become more efficient.

Headcount Is the Default Answer

Operational surges trigger requests for more seats instead of root-cause fixes.

Focus Narrows to Cost per Hour

The client negotiates hourly rates instead of measuring cost per successful outcome.

Rethinking the economics

Change the Measure and the Behavior Changes.

Handle Time vs. Outcome

Rushing to hit a short-handle-time target can create repeat work. The right measure is the customer outcome, not the clock.

Cost per Resolution

A problem resolved in one interaction is cheaper than the same problem requiring several, even when the per-interaction cost looks lower.

Value-Based Automation

Automation handles repeatable tasks so human judgment is reserved for complex sales, support, and retention conversations.

Shared Upside

When compensation connects to results, the provider has a direct reason to find ways to create more value.

Frequently Asked Questions About Hourly BPO Pricing

Why is hourly BPO pricing a problem?
Hourly pricing primarily rewards the delivery of labor. The provider is paid based on time worked even when business outcomes such as conversion, retention, or revenue do not improve.
Does hourly pricing always mean poor performance?
No. Many strong providers use hourly pricing. The issue is incentive alignment, not an assumption that hourly providers perform poorly.
Is pay-for-performance always better than hourly pricing?
No. Performance pricing works best when outcomes can be clearly defined, measured, attributed, and meaningfully influenced by the provider.
Why do most BPOs use hourly pricing?
Hourly pricing is simple, predictable, and closely tied to the provider's primary operating cost, which is labor.
Can customer service use performance-based pricing?
Yes. Compensation can be tied to outcomes such as first-contact resolution, repeat-contact reduction, customer satisfaction, and retention when reliable measurement is available.
Can sales outsourcing be paid based on revenue?
Yes. When attribution and the sales process allow it, compensation can be connected to revenue or other agreed outcomes the operation can reasonably influence.
Does SONIQCX still charge operating fees?
SONIQCX does not charge clients by the hour. Compensation is tied to the agreed performance outcomes established for the engagement. The exact commercial structure depends on the program.
How do you measure performance fairly?
The client and SONIQCX define the outcome, source of truth, baseline, attribution rules, exclusions, and verification process before the program begins.
What happens when the client controls part of the result?
Performance terms should account for client-controlled factors such as pricing, inventory, fulfillment, final sales execution, and product quality.
Can an existing hourly BPO be converted to performance pricing?
Potentially. The first step is establishing reliable performance measurement and identifying which outcomes can reasonably be connected to provider activity.
