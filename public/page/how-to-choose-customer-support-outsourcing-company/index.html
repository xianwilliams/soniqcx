The evaluation framework

Thirteen Questions to Answer Before You Pick a Provider

Define the Customer Problem

Start with what customers actually need help with, not with what a vendor sells.

Define the Support Scope

Establish which issues, channels, and responsibilities are included.

Define Successful Resolution

Agree what "solved" means for each issue category.

Define Escalation

Clarify what moves to another team and what context travels with it.

Define Provider Authority

Establish what the provider can decide and do without asking.

Define Systems and Data

Agree what the provider can access and use to help the customer.

Define Measurement

Establish how the support outcome is tracked and verified.

Define Attribution

Agree how results connect to the operation's work.

Ask How AI Is Used

Understand what is automated and what requires a person.

Ask What Requires a Human

Confirm the provider keeps human judgment for empathy and exceptions.

Compare Commercial Models

Understand whether you are paying for hours, seats, or outcomes.

Ask How Disputes Are Reconciled

Know what happens when a result is questioned.

Ask How Improvement Is Measured

Understand how the provider shows it is getting better over time.

Vendor evaluation checklist

Questions to Put in Front of Any Provider

  • Can the provider define the support scope and issue categories clearly?
  • Does the provider distinguish support quality from raw activity metrics?
  • Is escalation defined, and does context travel with the handoff?
  • What systems and data does the provider need, and can you provide them?
  • How are outcomes measured, and using what source of truth?
  • How does the provider use AI, and what is kept human?
  • Is the commercial model tied to hours, seats, or outcomes?
  • How are disputed results reconciled?
  • How does the provider demonstrate improvement over time?

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I choose a customer support outsourcing company?
Start by defining the customer problem, support scope, resolution, escalation, and measurement, then evaluate providers against that definition rather than against a generic feature list.
What is the most important thing to define first?
The customer problem and what a successful resolution means. Everything else , scope, measurement, and commercial model , follows from that.
Should I choose the cheapest provider?
Not necessarily. The right question is whether the commercial model is aligned with the outcome you want, not simply whether the hourly rate is the lowest.
Why does the commercial model matter?
It determines what the provider is economically rewarded for. A provider paid by the hour is rewarded for time; a provider paid on outcomes is rewarded for results.
Is SONIQCX the best customer support outsourcing company?
We do not claim to be objectively "the best." What distinguishes SONIQCX is that we do not charge clients by the hour , compensation is tied to agreed performance outcomes.
What should I ask a provider about AI?
Ask what is automated, what requires a human, how AI escalates to a person, and how AI use affects the customer outcome.
How do I know a provider is improving?
Ask how the provider measures improvement over time and what data it uses to prove it, rather than relying on activity reports.
